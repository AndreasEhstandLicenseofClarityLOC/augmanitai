# Human–AI work: from a thought to something that changes

**Andreas Ehstand · AUGMANITAI**

An idea becomes a conversation. The conversation becomes a picture, a piece of software, a decision or a shared task. Something happens outside the conversation, and that response changes the next step.

AUGMANITAI's ambition is to make this whole process easier to understand, develop and carry across people, models and tools. These public works connect performance coaching, human–AI dialogue, creative work, experiential knowledge, multi-model thinking, agents and robotics.

This guide offers several ways in. Each leads to an original publication or an existing public collection.

## 1. The person who works with several models

What changes when someone develops a sustained way of thinking with several AI models, while keeping their own judgment and testing ideas against consequences outside the chat?

[Read the conceptual definition of AUGMANITAI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23279129).

## 2. Performance across the whole working process

An impressive answer is one moment in a longer process. The human still frames the task, notices mistakes, chooses what matters and decides what to do next. How can those contributions become visible?

[Four practical questions for analysing your own performance when working with AI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20613914) transfers a performance-coaching perspective to demanding human–AI work.

## 3. Agent teams and collective results

Several agents, tools and human decisions can contribute to the same outcome. Where should we draw the boundary around the team before describing its performance?

[Swarms and Multi-Agent Collectives](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21990198) explores that question as a conceptual note.

## 4. What a human actually checked

Seeing an output, checking a property, choosing an action and authorising it are different acts. Recording that distinction can make the question of human oversight more precise.

[Terminology for Human Oversight Acts in Automated and Agentic Systems](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ehstand-oversight-acts/) is an individual Internet-Draft offered for discussion, rather than an adopted IETF standard.

## 5. Experience that another person can use

What needs to travel with a decision so that someone else can understand the thinking behind it?

[Gedankenvererbungslehre](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22211952) develops a conceptual framework for the continuation of thought. The practical article [Debriefing: Wie Sie Erfahrungswissen übergeben](https://www.business-wissen.de/artikel/debriefing-wie-sie-erfahrungswissen-uebergeben/) addresses the handover of experiential knowledge.

## 6. Language, definitions and changing meaning

People and software may use the same word while relying on different definitions. A later revision adds another question: which version shaped this text or decision?

[Explore the bilingual public lexicon](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22228966) and the article [When a definition changes: which version did the AI-assisted text use?](https://kminsider.com/km-and-ai/definition-versioning-ai-assisted-text/).

## 7. What people experience in a dialogue

Feeling understood is an experience worth describing carefully. How could observations of a conversation and a person's own account be distinguished and studied?

[Feeling Understood by Conversational AI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22871354) proposes a taxonomy and a research hypothesis.

## 9. From software teams to embodied systems

When robots enter a working arrangement, the world can answer through physical contact: a gripper meets an object or empty space. Which parts of the human–AI–robot arrangement belong in its description?

[The Robot-Fleet Operator Unit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21990196) develops this as a conceptual transfer to robotics.
